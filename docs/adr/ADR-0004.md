# Use Figma for UX Storyboarding and Wireframes

* Status: accepted
* Date: 2026-10-21
* Deciders: HCDD 412 Team 1

## Context and Problem Statement

Team 1 is developing a web-based Heart Rate Calculator. For the Week 9 storyboard deliverable, the team needs to represent three user flows and connect each storyboard frame to a user story and its testable acceptance criteria on the Agile board. The deliverable also requires UX annotations, a heuristic and accessibility review, documented AI use, and a shareable Figma link or PDF export, and which tool the team uses to create and share the storyboard and wireframes.

## Decision Drivers

* Make each frame traceable to its user story by putting the story number at the start of the frame name.
* Organize the screens for three user flows and allow the team to revise them.
* Add annotations explaining the story served, design decision, and relevant usability heuristic.
* Support a review against Nielsen's ten usability heuristics and text contrast of at least 4.5:1 for normal text under WCAG AA.
* Allow the storyboard to be shared by view link or exported to PDF.
* Support AI-assisted drafting while allowing the team to review and document prompts and changes.

## Considered Options

* **Figma (Path A):** Create a design file with one page per flow and story-numbered frames.
* **Mermaid diagrams and plain-text wireframes (Path B):** Create text-based journeys, screen-flow diagrams, and wireframes in Markdown.
* **Another diagramming or drawing tool:** Create and export wireframes using a tool such as draw.io or Lucidchart, or use hand-drawn wireframes.

## Decision Outcome

Chosen option: Figma. The team will use Figma to create the Week 9 storyboard and wireframes because its page-and-frame workflow makes it practical to organize the three flows, label each frame with its related story number, annotate design decisions, and share or export the results.

The Figma file will have one page per flow. Each frame name will begin with its story number (for example, `S12-R1 Request a ride`, adapted to the team's actual story numbers). The corresponding frame link will be added to the relevant Agile-board issue description or comment, and the issue will receive a `ux` label or equivalent tag. The team will capture a board screenshot showing at least three stories with their frame links.

The team will use Figma AI for initial drafts if the feature is available. If it is not available or is limited, the team will use another AI tool or create the screens manually. Each flow will be revised at least once. The team will keep a log of prompts and changes, add annotations, review all ten of Nielsen's heuristics, and record a text-contrast check. The final file will be shared with view access or exported as a PDF; a shared link will be tested in a private browser window.

### Consequences

* Good: The storyboard will be visually organized and easier to revise and review. Story-numbered frames and Agile-board links will make the connection between the design, user story, and acceptance criteria easier to verify. Annotations and export/share options support the lab's submission requirements.
* Bad: Team members need access to Figma and must manage sharing permissions. Story links, heuristic findings, contrast results, and AI prompt/revision notes require manual upkeep. If Figma AI is unavailable, the team must use another drafting method and document it. Wireframes communicate intended behavior but do not replace testing the implemented calculator against its acceptance criteria.
